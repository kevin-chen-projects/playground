The big idea: a cell is a tiny city.

That's it. That's the whole metaphor. A cell has walls, factories, a power plant, a library, and neighborhoods — just way smaller, and made of molecules instead of bricks.

🧱→🦠
City walls / Cell membrane

Decides what gets in and out. Wraps the whole cell like a stretchy bubble.

📚→🧬
Library / Nucleus

Stores the instructions (DNA) for everything the cell builds.

⚡→🔋
Power plants / Mitochondria

Turn food into energy the cell can actually use.

🏭→🟡
Factories / Ribosomes

Read instructions from the library and build proteins.

💧→🌊
Neighborhoods / Cytoplasm

The jelly-like fluid that fills the cell. Where everything floats and happens.

Animal cell vs. plant cell.

Animal and plant cells are mostly the same — same membrane, same nucleus, same mitochondria. But plants have a few extras that let them stand tall and make food from sunlight.

🦠 Cell membrane

Both have one. Plant cells have it too, just behind the wall.

🧱 Cell wall (plant only)

A stiff outer layer made of cellulose. It's why plants don't flop over.

☀️ Chloroplasts (plant only)

Tiny solar panels. Catch sunlight and turn it into sugar (photosynthesis).

💧 Big vacuole (plant only)

A water balloon that takes up most of the cell. Helps the plant stay firm.
