Now we actually build the protein.

You have an mRNA strand sitting next to a ribosome outside the nucleus. The ribosome doesn't read English. It reads codons: 3-letter chunks of mRNA.

Each codon picks one amino acid. The amino acids snap together into a chain. The chain folds up — and that's a working protein.

Module 4 of 11 · Scene 1 of 6

3 letters → 1 amino acid.

The genetic code links codons (mRNA words) to amino acids (protein building blocks). It's the same in bacteria, plants, fungi, and you. Pretty incredible.

📜
The codon

A 3-letter word in mRNA. Like AUG, UCA, GGC. There are 64 possible.

🧱
The amino acid

A small molecule. There are 20 different ones — chained, they make proteins.

🚚
tRNA (delivery truck)

For each codon, a tRNA shows up carrying the matching amino acid.

🏭
Ribosome (assembly line)

Reads mRNA, accepts tRNAs, links amino acids together.

📐 What's protein folding?

Once the amino acid chain is built, it folds into a 3D shape — like crumpling a string into a specific knot. That shape is what makes the protein actually do something. Hemoglobin's shape catches oxygen. An antibody's shape grips a virus. Insulin's shape signals your liver to take in sugar.
