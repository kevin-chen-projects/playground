2 Targets & abundance

List each antibody target and estimate how abundant it is in your sample. Bright dyes go to rare targets, dim dyes to abundant ones — so no single signal drowns out the rest.

How the assignment works

Observed signal in a channel is roughly antigen abundance × dye brightness. The optimizer:

  1. Balances signal — pairs bright fluorophores (e.g. AF647) with your rarest targets and dim ones (e.g. AF405) with abundant targets, so every channel lands near a common brightness. This is the dominant objective.
  2. Separates spectra — assigns one dye per band and penalizes leaning on spectrally-adjacent channels (488→561, 561→640) when the donor signal is strong.
  3. Avoids autofluorescence — keeps rare, sensitivity-critical targets off the green (488) and violet bands where tissue background is highest.

With targets ≤ channels the search is exhaustive, so the result is the exact optimum for this model.

Heads-up: FluoroMatch is a planning aid, not a substitute for empirical validation. Brightness and abundance are relative estimates — real performance depends on your antibody titer, conjugation ratio, fixation, mounting medium, detector sensitivity, and exact filter sets. Always validate single-stain controls and check for spillover on your own system before trusting a multiplex panel.
