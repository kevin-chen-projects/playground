Welcome to the most audible part of your accent.

Your native language has a small, tidy vowel system. American English has a sprawling one — about 12 distinct vowels, and to a native ear they're as different as red and green.

Tap any card. Do these two words sound the same to you, or different? If "the same" — that's the gap we're going to close.

Module 1 of 1 · Scene 1 of 6

The American English vowel space.

Linguists arrange vowels by where your tongue sits in your mouth. Front vowels are made near the front of your mouth (smile shape). Back vowels are made near the back (lip-round shape). High means tongue near the roof; low means jaw open. Tap any vowel to hear it on the right.

Scene 3 of 6 · Confusable pairs are next

Minimal pairs — where accents reveal themselves.

A "minimal pair" is two real English words that differ by exactly one vowel: sheep vs ship, bed vs bad. Native ears hear the split instantly. The card outlined in coral is the pair speakers of your first language confuse most often. Tap any card to hear both.
