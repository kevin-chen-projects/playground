Module 1 of 10 · Scene 1 of 6

The big idea: money is whatever everyone agrees it is.

That's it. That's the whole secret. For thousands of years humans have used wildly different things as money — as long as enough people agreed it had value, it worked.

10,000 BCE 🐔
Barter

I have a chicken, you have shoes. Let's trade. Problem: what if you don't want my chicken?

3,000 BCE 🐚
Shells & salt

People picked rare-but-handy things everyone wanted. Cowrie shells were "money" across most of Africa & Asia.

600 BCE 🪙
Coins

Lydia (modern-day Turkey) stamped the first official coins. Big upgrade: governments guaranteed the weight.

~1,000 CE 📜
Paper money

China invented it. Way easier to carry. The bill itself is just a promise: "this is worth real stuff."

Today 📱
Digital

Most money lives as numbers in bank databases. You almost never touch the "real" thing — you just move digits.

Cash vs. digital money. Same job, very different vibes.

When you buy a $5 coffee, you can either hand the cashier a paper bill or tap your phone. Both work. But the two systems are wildly different under the hood.

👀 Anonymous

Nobody tracks who spent what. The bill doesn't remember its journey.

🔋 No battery needed

Works in a power outage. Works without internet. Works always.

😬 Easy to lose

Drop it, leave it on the bus, get pickpocketed — it's just gone.

⚡ Instant + global

Send $50 to a friend across the world in seconds. Cash can't do that.

🧾 Fully tracked

Every swipe is logged. Banks, apps, and stores all keep records.

🛑 Can be frozen

Banks can freeze your account. Apps can ban you. Cash in a sock can't.

Scene 3 of 6

Trading time vs. building income that scales.

Most jobs trade time directly for money. Stop showing up, money stops. Some careers/businesses break that link — building something once and earning many times. Both are valid; just know which one you're in.

📅 Predictable

Show up, get paid. Boring but reliable. Most W-2 jobs work this way.

🪜 Linear

2× the hours = 2× the pay (at best). There's a hard ceiling: 168 hours in a week.

🛌 Stops if you stop

Get sick, take leave, retire — income drops fast. No accumulating asset.

📚 Build once, sell many

A book, course, software product, business — write/build it once, get paid by every customer who finds it.

🚀 No ceiling

10 customers and 10,000 customers cost similar effort. The upside is uncapped.

🎲 Risky upfront

Most scalable bets fail. The few that work do absurdly well. Have a stable income while you experiment.

Coming up next: Banks & accounts

So you've earned money. Where does it actually go when you put it in a bank? (Spoiler: not into a vault.) We'll demystify checking, savings, and the absurdly underrated high-yield savings account.

Module complete · Banks is unlocked
~10%
— how much of deposits banks legally have to keep on hand.

Your money isn't actually in the vault.

When you deposit $1,000, the bank lends $900 of it to someone else the same day. They earn interest on the loan and pay you a tiny bit. Multiply that across millions of customers — that's the whole banking business.

Mostly fine. Banks are FDIC-insured, your money's safe. But understanding the system is what unlocks better choices — like why online banks pay 100× more interest than the big ones.

Module 4 of 10 · Scene 1 of 6

The big idea: a bank is a middleman.

The bank's whole business is: borrow from you cheap, lend to others expensive, keep the difference. This 5-step flow runs the world's largest industry.

STEP 1 💵
You deposit

$1,000 of yours goes into the bank. Feels safe — and is. FDIC insurance covers it up to $250K.

STEP 2 🏗️
Bank lends $900

To a homebuyer, business, or another customer. They charge that borrower 6-22% interest.

STEP 3 🪙
Bank earns

Interest from the loan. Subtracts costs (your tiny interest, branch rent, salaries).

STEP 4 🤝
You get $5/year

Big-bank savings rate is often 0.4%. They pocket the spread between what they charge and what they pay.

STEP 5 ⚙️
It works

Because not everyone wants their money at once. The system depends on this — and on FDIC trust.

Compound interest is the 8th wonder of the world.

(At least according to a quote attributed to Einstein, who probably never said it. But the math is real.)

Compound interest is when your money earns money, and then THAT money earns money. It feels boring for the first 10 years — and then becomes obscene in years 30-50. The trick is starting early.

Module 6 of 10 · Scene 1 of 6

The big idea: APR means rent.

When you borrow $1,000 at 20% APR, you're effectively renting that money for $200/year. If you don't pay back the principal, the rent just keeps going. Watch how this snowballs:

YEAR 0 💳
Borrow $1,000

You spend it. APR is 20%. Sounds harmless, like a small fee.

YEAR 1 📈
Owe $1,200

You didn't pay. Now $200 in interest got added. Next year's rent is on $1,200.

YEAR 2 ⚠️
Owe $1,440

The interest is now compounding. $240 added this year, not $200.

YEAR 5 🚨
Owe $2,488

You've barely been paying it off. Or worse — only paying minimums. Interest >> principal payments.

YEAR 10 💀
Owe $6,191

The original $1,000 has grown 6×. This is reverse compound interest — and it's the credit card industry's whole business model.

Scene 3 of 6

Good debt vs. bad debt.

Not all debt is bad. The trick is asking: "Does this borrowing make me richer or poorer?" If the answer is "richer over time," it might be worth it.

🏠 Mortgage on a home you'll keep

Low interest, asset usually appreciates, builds equity. Often the only way to own anything big.

🎓 Student loan with high ROI

If the degree clearly raises your earning power. Federal loans have flexible repayment.

💼 Smart business loan

Borrowing $50K at 7% to start a business that earns $80K/year. Math wins.

🚗 New luxury car loan

High rate, depreciating asset, no income generated. Loses 20% of value on day 1.

💳 Carrying credit card balance

20%+ APR. Almost always for consumption (vacations, dinners). Reverse compounding starts immediately.

⚠️ Payday loan

Effective APR 300%+. The single worst financial decision available. Almost always there's a better option.

Scene 3 of 6

Picking stocks vs. just buying everything.

The most counterintuitive lesson in investing: trying harder makes you do worse. The boring "just buy everything" strategy beats the experts over decades.

🎰 Stress + research

Watch every news cycle, panic on dips, buy on hype. Constant stress, often worse returns.

📉 ~10% beat the market

Among professional fund managers, ~90% underperform the S&P 500 over 20 years. Amateurs do worse.

💸 Higher fees

Active trading triggers more taxes and (sometimes) higher fund fees. Drag on returns.

📦 Buy "the whole market"

One purchase = ownership of every major company. Auto-diversified. No research required.

💎 Tiny fees

VTI (Vanguard Total Market) charges 0.03%/year. On $100K, that's $30/year. Compare: actively managed funds often charge 1-2%.

🛌 Set + forget

Auto-buy every paycheck. Don't watch the news. The boring strategy is the winning strategy.

Module 8 of 10 · Scene 1 of 6

The big idea: brackets are a staircase, not a cliff.

Take a single filer earning $80,000 in 2024. Look at how each chunk of income gets taxed at a different rate. You're never penalized for earning more.

STEP 1 🟢
$0 - $11,600

10% taxed. The first $11,600 of every American's income is taxed at the same low rate.

STEP 2 🟡
$11,601 - $47,150

12%. The dollars in this band are taxed at 12% — but the dollars in step 1 are still only taxed at 10%.

STEP 3 🟠
$47,151 - $100,525

22%. Our $80K earner has dollars in this band — but only those dollars hit 22%.

RESULT 🧮
Total: ~$13,000

Effective tax rate: ~16% — even though the top bracket they touched was 22%. Brackets ≠ what you actually pay.

Scene 3 of 6

W-2 employee vs. 1099 contractor.

Same dollar earned — radically different tax life. Employees have everything done for them. Freelancers have more control AND more responsibility.

🤖 Auto-withholding

Employer takes federal, state, and FICA out of every paycheck. April is mostly auto-pilot.

🛡️ FICA split with employer

You pay 7.65% — employer matches the other 7.65%. Real benefit, not always obvious.

📉 Limited deductions

The standard deduction (~$14,600 single) is usually the easy choice. Few extra write-offs.

📅 Quarterly estimated payments

Pay the IRS 4 times a year (April, June, Sept, Jan). Miss a payment, get penalties.

💸 Self-employment tax (15.3%)

You pay BOTH halves of FICA. Surprise hit for first-time freelancers.

🧾 Lots of deductions

Home office, software, mileage, equipment, health insurance. Tracked + deducted, can save thousands.

Track every dollar vs. set and forget.

Two valid approaches. Some people love spreadsheets and tracking apps. Others would rather dental work. Both work — pick the one you'll actually do.

📊 Maximum visibility

You know exactly where every dollar goes. No surprises. Easy to find leaks.

⏰ Time-intensive

💪 Behavior-changing

The act of categorizing makes you spend less. The receipt has weight when you have to log it.

🤖 Automate the wins

🧠 Less decision fatigue

You don't have to think. The structure handles it. Saves mental energy for actual life.

🎯 Target the leaks

Quarterly subscription audit, automated bill pay. Don't track every coffee — but DO catch the $14.99/mo apps.

Real opportunity vs. scam.
