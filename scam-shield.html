🚩 The 6 biggest warning signs

If a message has even one of these, slow down. Real companies and real family members do not behave this way.

!
It rushes you. "Act now or your account closes in 24 hours."
!
It scares you. Threats of arrest, fines, or losing your money.
!
It asks for secrets. Passwords, PINs, or codes — no real company asks.
!
It wants odd payment. Gift cards, wire transfers, or cryptocurrency.
!
It comes out of nowhere. A prize you didn't enter, a refund you weren't owed.
!
The details are off. Odd spelling, a strange web address, "Dear Customer."

Lesson

It's going to be okay. Let's take it one step at a time.

Being targeted is not your fault — these criminals fool millions of people every year, including careful, smart people. Acting quickly can limit the harm. Here's exactly what to do.

1

Stop all contact

Don't send any more money, don't click anything else, and don't answer if they call back. It's okay to simply hang up or stop replying.

2

Call your bank right away

Use the phone number on the back of your card — not a number anyone gave you. Tell them what happened. They can freeze cards, stop transfers, and watch for fraud.

3

Change your passwords

Start with email and banking. If you reused that password anywhere else, change it there too. Ask a trusted person to help if you'd like.

4

Write down what happened

Note dates, amounts, phone numbers, and any names. This helps your bank and the authorities.

5

Report it

Reporting helps stop the scammer from hurting someone else. Use the contacts below.

6

Tell someone you trust

A family member or friend can help you watch your accounts and feel less alone. You have nothing to be embarrassed about.

Who to call (United States)

Your bank or card
Number on your card

Report fraud and freeze accounts. Available 24/7 at most banks.

Federal Trade Commission
ReportFraud.ftc.gov

The U.S. government's official place to report scams. Or call 1-877-382-4357.

Social Security fraud
1-800-269-0271

If a caller claimed to be from Social Security or threatened your benefits.

Local police
Non-emergency line

File a report, especially if you lost money. Call 911 only for emergencies.
